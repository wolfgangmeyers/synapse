# Spec: Claude Mail Startup Recovery

- **ID**: `SPEC-CLAUDE-START-RETRY-001`
- **Status**: `Draft` — proposed behavior; implementation and acceptance pending
- **Domain**: `Mailbox`
- **E2E Suites**: `tests/e2e/claude-worker-start-settlement.spec.ts`, `tests/e2e/claude-start-preflight-privacy.spec.ts` and `tests/e2e/claude-start-retry-*.spec.ts`

## 1. Intent & User Outcome

A Claude worker automatically recovers accepted mail from transient startup failures without a new ping. Persistent failures produce a useful, sanitized Complete-history entry, while router logs retain the cause for diagnosis. This contract applies only to Claude mail definitely not submitted to stdin; other runtimes and uncertain submissions retain their existing behavior.

## 2. Core Invariants

1. A category never authorizes replay: retry only a confirmed definite pre-stdin failure after required cleanup. Started or uncertain writes remain held, never replayed or falsely settled as unsubmitted. Unknown safety evidence fails closed. Process fences and cleanup requirements remain in force.
2. Each message ID, incarnation and recipient has one fixed deadline, 6m35s after its first definite failure, and at most five executions including the initial execution. Subsequent failures, offers, restarts and safe owner rebinding never extend the deadline or reset the count. Retries after failures 1–4 start no earlier than 5s, 30s, 2m and 3m after the respective failure. Confirmed auth and confirmed missing executable settle promptly without retries.
3. No retry starts at or after the deadline. If its next eligible time is at or beyond the deadline, wait for the deadline and settle without execution. After failure five, wait for the deadline before settling. A safely started execution may finish after the deadline; accept success, apply normal uncertain/cleanup handling, and settle a definite failure after cleanup if the deadline has passed. No timer settles concurrently with an in-flight attempt.
4. Persist the deadline, next eligibility, execution count and last sanitized category for the exact message incarnation and recipient. Durably admit the initial execution and every retry before execution. An unresolved admission, including a crash or failure to persist its first failure, remains in flight and cannot authorize replay or settlement; the retry deadline begins at the first definite failure. Revalidate owner, accepted envelope, attempt and stdin/consumption evidence transactionally on mutation, admission and settlement. Persistence failure fails closed. A crash during a retry does not prove it unsubmitted or grant another attempt.
5. Log the full underlying error with agent ID, message ID and attempt number for each definite start failure. Complete history uses only allowlisted `auth`, `spawn`, `probe-died`, `pool-fenced`, `grant-failed` or `unknown` and a category-specific actionable hint; unknown values fall back to `unknown`. Raw paths, secrets and provider output never appear in that entry.
6. Settlement is atomic, fenced and idempotent. Storage failure retries settlement only, retaining the last category; it may delay history and dead-letter beyond the deadline. Stale fences never alter a replacement. Publish only after commit; no duplicate history on rollback or ambiguous commit.
7. Provider selection is serialized from the model snapshot through the retry-state check and any durable admission, so a model change cannot enter between them. Provider changes and mid-turn steering cannot bypass existing Claude retry admission, eligibility, or an unresolved in-flight fence. A different provider cannot execute retry-bearing mail; it remains blocked until safely resolved under the Claude contract. A definite pre-submission input-bounds rejection releases its reservation and associated admission atomically, only after cleanup and safety checks. Prompt auth or confirmed missing-executable disposition is persisted with the failure and survives restart; ordinary spawn failures do not become prompt-settleable.
When the provider changes while retry state is waiting, prompt disposition settles promptly and ordinary waiting state wakes at its original deadline for settlement; no different provider executes it. An unresolved in-flight state remains blocked. A process fence caused by unconfirmed cleanup is not a definite failure. Rejected-input release must revalidate the exact accepted envelope and message incarnation and roll back unless the exact safe in-flight admission is cleared together with its reservation. If a rejected retry is released, retain the existing failure and schedule a wake at its original deadline for settlement, without an immediate automatic retry of the invalid input.

8. A transient retry-state read or admission storage error schedules a storage recheck after a bounded delay; it never authorizes execution. Rechecks re-read durable state and obey the original deadline and eligibility, including after an ambiguous admission. A stale fence does not schedule a storage retry. At most one retry timer per lane. Clocks supporting `AfterFuncAt` register the absolute eligibility deadline; already-due timers wake without another advance. Relative-only clocks use a post-registration deadline check, but can wake late if they advance during registration without reaching the deadline. Waits release serial locks, every offer and wake respects persisted eligibility, and shutdown cancels timers. Retry state survives shutdown and safe owner rebinding; delete it transactionally on success, terminal settlement, expiry/deletion or replacement, with message expiry as a cleanup backstop, subject to the limitations below. Pi and other runtimes are unchanged.

An unclassified result from a dispatched Claude mail execution has a durable in-flight admission before the provider is entered. A context reset may retire its reservation, but must not erase that admission or interpret the absence of an stdin marker as proof of non-submission. A replacement owner, including one using a different provider, must not execute or ACK that incarnation while the admission is unresolved. A stale diagnostic is discarded rather than written under the replacement's identity. A reservation alone, without an admission, does not establish that a Claude execution was entered; synthetic tests must distinguish that state from an unclassified execution.

**Retention and deletion assessment:** Normal accepted mail receives an expiry timestamp one hour after router acceptance, not one hour after the client-provided creation time; reminder mail also receives one hour. This is a TTL, not a hard one-hour retention upper bound for retry data. The normal periodic sweep runs by default every second and deletes expired queued mail only when unclaimed and not frozen by managed termination; recovery uses the same eligibility. Router downtime, sweep/storage failure, a claim or a termination freeze can delay deletion without a proven finite wall-clock upper bound. Retry rows have no independent age-based pruning. Message deletion cascades to retry rows; an update changing incarnation or recipient, or moving state away from queued, deletes them via a database trigger. Success and terminal settlement use those lifecycle paths. The public delete endpoint dismisses dead letters, not waiting queued mail; direct SQLite deletion/replacement in the retention tests does not establish a public waiting-mail deletion path. Consequently the required bounded-retention guarantee remains an open gap.

### Scenario: Reserved head and concurrent offers
- **Given**: Older queued mail is reserved and later mail is unreserved, or many offers arrive while serial dispatch is busy.
- **When**: A live runtime is offered mail or serial fallback is needed.
- **Then**: Later mail cannot steer past the reserved head. Offers coalesce to a bounded serial waiter, with a subsequent wake for offers arriving during dispatch. Retry-bearing mail still goes through serial admission, and replacement runtime wakes remain independent.

### Scenario: A live runtime is replaced during an offer
- **Given**: A mail offer targets a live runtime that is replaced before steering acquires its submission gate.
- **When**: The replacement remains active and mail is eligible for steering.
- **Then**: The offer is rechecked against the replacement without waiting for its turn to release the serial lane. Retry-bearing mail still requires serial admission.

## 3. User Scenarios (Behavioral Contract)

### Scenario: [CSR-001] Transient failure recovers automatically
- **User Goal**: Have accepted mail delivered without intervention.
- **Given**: The fake Claude CLI definitely fails before stdin on the first one or two executions, then succeeds.
- **When**: Time advances through the applicable retry eligibility, without a new message or ping.
- **Then**: The same mail is delivered once and answered; no terminal failure is recorded. Repeated offers and settlement timer wakes do not start it early or move the deadline. Each failed attempt is logged with its full cause, agent, message and attempt number; retry state is removed on success.

### Scenario: [CSR-002] Persistent failure reaches the fixed deadline
- **User Goal**: See a bounded, actionable failure instead of an idle worker.
- **Given**: Every execution definitely fails before stdin and cleanup succeeds.
- **When**: Time advances through all eligibility points and the original deadline, including a case where an eligible time is at or beyond the deadline.
- **Then**: At most five executions occur with the specified delays; none starts at or after the deadline. Even if five fail earlier, settlement waits for the deadline. One sanitized category-specific Complete-history entry and dead letter appear for the accepted incarnation, then later eligible mail is offered. Logs include each full cause and identifying attempt. Retry state is removed.

### Scenario: [CSR-003] Non-transient preflight failures
- **User Goal**: Fix an installation or login issue promptly.
- **Given**: Claude preflight explicitly parses `loggedIn: false`, or confirms the executable is missing.
- **When**: A mail acquisition definitely fails before stdin.
- **Then**: It settles promptly without backoff, respectively as `auth` or `spawn`, with an appropriate sanitized hint. Missing-executable disposition requires explicit confirmation during executable resolution; an ENOENT from a missing working directory, script interpreter, or other spawn boundary alone does not confirm a missing executable and must follow the normal retry schedule if safety is proven. Such spawn errors retain the `spawn` category. The launch API retains its existing admission behavior. An ambiguous preflight timeout, nonzero command exit, malformed, missing or overflowed JSON is `unknown`, not auth; retain a real or non-sensitive synthesized cause and retry only if definite-start safety is proven.

### Scenario: [CSR-004] Categories and diagnostic privacy
- **User Goal**: Distinguish startup problems without exposing sensitive details in history.
- **Given**: Independent definite failures arise at spawn, startup probe death, process fence, grant creation and an unrecognized boundary, with injected secret and path in the underlying cause.
- **When**: Each failure is logged and safely reaches terminal settlement.
- **Then**: Its category is respectively `spawn`, `probe-died`, `pool-fenced`, `grant-failed`, or `unknown`, with an appropriate allowlisted hint; an unrecognized category also falls back to `unknown`. Full causes and agent/message/attempt identifiers are in router logs, not in Complete history. A fence that prevents safe execution remains fenced or held, not converted into permission to retry or settle.

### Scenario: [CSR-005] Restart and owner changes preserve the window
- **User Goal**: Recover waiting mail without another ping or resetting its retry budget.
- **Given**: A message is waiting for retry, or a retry was recorded as started, and its durable state is retained.
- **When**: The router restarts before eligibility, after deadline, during an attempt, or the owner legitimately rebinds.
- **Then**: Recovery schedules remaining waiting work without new mail, using the original deadline and count. It revalidates the current owner, exact envelope, attempt and stdin/consumption markers. After deadline it settles only if safe; an in-flight crash or unsafe takeover remains blocked/held under existing recovery fences, never presumed unsubmitted. A different incarnation never inherits the state.

### Scenario: [CSR-006] In-flight execution crosses deadline
- **User Goal**: Keep a valid result while avoiding unsafe overlapping settlement.
- **Given**: A safe retry starts before the deadline and remains in flight at the deadline.
- **When**: It later succeeds, definitely fails after cleanup, or has an uncertain write or cleanup failure in separate cases.
- **Then**: No deadline timer settles concurrently. Success is accepted; definite failure settles after cleanup; uncertain or cleanup outcomes retain their existing hold/fence handling without replay.

### Scenario: [CSR-007] Settlement and persistence failures
- **User Goal**: Avoid duplicate delivery or history when storage is unreliable.
- **Given**: Definite failure reaches terminal settlement, or retry-state persistence fails, with fault injection for rollback, ambiguous commit and stale owner/attempt/incarnation.
- **When**: Storage recovers or a replacement appears.
- **Then**: No execution is retried because of a storage failure; settlement alone is retried with the last category, at most one timer per lane. There is at most one committed history entry and no mutation of a replacement. A stale fence is not treated as a storage retry. Subsequent eligible mail is offered after settlement or stale disposition. Publication follows commit.

### Scenario: [CSR-008] Held mail, cleanup and retention
- **User Goal**: Preserve safety and avoid accumulating retry state.
- **Given**: Separate cases have a started or uncertain stdin write, an unclassified failure with no stdin marker, unknown marker state, failed cleanup, or a waiting message that expires, is deleted or replaced.
- **When**: Offers, restart, reset and concurrent wakeups occur, or the message reaches its lifecycle end.
- **Then**: Uncertain mail remains held; unclassified or unknown-safety cases follow existing blocked/diagnostic behavior, without inventing a marker or retry authorization. Cleanup failure retains the process fence. No stale action touches a replacement; expiry/deletion/replacement removes old retry state. Waiting releases the lane and shutdown cancels timers; unrelated eligible work follows existing safety rules.

Storage read failures while routing a wake or selecting embedded mail must hold delivery and schedule a bounded recheck without requiring another message. A pending settlement with a future store deadline schedules a bounded settlement-only recheck, including when manager and store clocks differ. An unresolved in-flight settlement stays dormant without recurring wakes; neither case authorizes execution.

Owner-claim storage faults also schedule a bounded recheck; a stale owner fence does not. Routing a wake must not wait for a lane already held by its caller. A deleted agent is terminal for wake scheduling. If the store rejects a retry admission because eligibility changed during admission, re-read durable state on a bounded wake: an expired safe retry settles, while an unresolved in-flight retry stays held without recurring wakes.

Absolute timer registration is guaranteed for clocks supporting `AfterFuncAt`. Relative-only custom clocks can advance between reading time and registering a timer, delaying a wake; the post-registration check catches only advances that reach the deadline during registration. Production uses the wall clock and relative timers, so ordinary scheduling latency can similarly delay a wake but cannot authorize early execution.

A confirmed cleaned-up failure whose recording fails remains held in memory and is retried by a bounded storage-only timer. Its exact admission, owner and envelope remain fenced; ambiguous commits are reconciled against the same execution count. Offers cannot execute while recording is pending. Shutdown discards the volatile proof: after a crash or restart an unresolved durable admission stays blocked, because cleanup cannot be inferred. The original failure time anchors the retry window even when storage recovery is delayed. Logs identify the execution attempt even if recording fails.

A cleaned-up, definitely rejected input whose release hits a transient storage error remains blocked in memory. Only the fenced release is retried on a bounded wake; no provider execution is repeated. A stale release is discarded and current mail is re-read. Shutdown or crash discards the volatile proof, leaving any unresolved durable admission in flight and fail-closed. After successful release of a retry, the original deadline still governs settlement.

### Failed-agent deadline recovery

If an independently rejected startup wake leaves an agent failed after a safely rejected retry released its reservation, a later offer or recovery wake must still settle the waiting mail at its original deadline without starting an execution. The retry row must retain the accepted snapshot identity, envelope digest, attempt and owner identity captured from a fenced attempt. The rejected-input release transaction records durable proof of safe release; rows lacking this evidence fail closed. In one transaction, settlement must recheck the exact message incarnation, recipient, accepted envelope and active target snapshot, failed agent identity, absence of a replacement reservation or consumption evidence, no stdin start, and a non-in-flight retry whose deadline has passed. A changed owner token, target, envelope, incarnation or reservation must not be touched. Startup recovery scans failed agents and schedules the same settlement-only wake; no new message is required. If the scan precedes a recovery wake that transitions an agent to failed, the committed failure transition itself triggers settlement scheduling without another offer or restart. A confirmed committed failure transition schedules the same wake even if the subsequent state-file permission check reports an error; a rolled-back or indeterminate transition must not schedule that failure-transition wake. The error remains visible in logs. A recovery owner can have a newer token than the owner that safely released the retry. The exact current owner’s fenced failure transaction transfers settlement authority for safely released, non-in-flight retries on that same session, epoch and generation to its own token; an independently changed token after that transaction remains stale. This transfer never authorizes execution and settlement must still recheck every message, target, reservation and consumption fence. The existing sanitized history, dead-letter transition and reminder side effects remain atomic and publish only after commit. A storage failure may delay settlement; it must not authorize execution. This does not resolve the bounded-retention gap described above.

Verification note: `claude-start-retry-rejected-input.spec.ts` passed both no-offer and offer/restart cases with a real rejected release and a synthetic SQLite failed-lifecycle transition. The separate `claude-start-retry-failed-recovery-wake.spec.ts` and `claude-start-retry-failed-transition-storage.spec.ts` passed in the 29-case run, covering an independent recovery wake and a rolled-back transition followed by recovery respectively; their fixtures still constrain the evidence. The retention gap remains open.

A startup owner failure transition retains its execution gate while its outcome is pending or indeterminate, even if the persisted lifecycle still says running. Mail, prompts, goal continuations and recovery must not start another execution under that owner. A confirmed commit, a conclusively superseded owner or shutdown releases the gate; an indeterminate outcome alone never does. A startup owner failure transition that conclusively rolls back on a storage error is retried as a storage-only operation after a bounded delay. Each attempt revalidates the original owner and launch operation; a stale fence or indeterminate transaction outcome stops the retry without a failure-transition wake. A confirmed commit wakes failed-agent settlement, including a post-commit permission error. Shutdown cancels pending retries; no retry starts a provider execution.

### Bounded retry metadata

The retry table admits at most 10,000 records and 16 MiB of logical metadata. Charged bytes are the UTF-8 bytes of every text field, except category is charged at least 12 bytes (the longest sanitized category), plus 8 bytes for each of the six integer fields. This reserves room at admission for any sanitized failure category. A transactional aggregate tracks row count and charged bytes; migration initializes it from all existing rows. Each record is also limited to 4,096 logical bytes. SQLite checks admission and growth in the same write transaction, including concurrent writers; failure is closed and existing retry evidence is not evicted. A full table or exhausted byte budget blocks new admissions and schedules the existing bounded storage recheck; it never grants execution. Deletion through the existing message lifecycle frees capacity.

Legacy databases may already exceed a limit. Migration preserves all rows, including unresolved or unsafe evidence. While over a limit, new admissions fail; existing rows may be updated only without increasing the violated byte limit. A legacy oversized row may not grow. These restrictions remain until ordinary safe lifecycle deletion frees enough capacity. No age-based eviction or assumption that an in-flight attempt is safe is permitted.

### Scenario: [CSR-009] Retry capacity and legacy evidence
- **User Goal**: Bound new retry metadata without losing evidence needed to prevent duplicate execution.
- **Given**: Capacity is exhausted, a candidate has oversized metadata, or legacy retry rows exceed a limit, including an unresolved admission.
- **When**: Mail attempts admission, retry state updates, capacity is freed, or the router restarts.
- **Then**: No write grows a violated bound, no new execution is admitted without a durable record, and no unsafe evidence is evicted. Competing admissions cannot exceed capacity. The existing bounded storage wake rechecks capacity without a new message, and admission resumes when sufficient capacity is safely freed. Storage faults also fail closed.

`claude-start-retry-capacity.spec.ts` exercises quota blocking and resumption through the browser, proxy, router and SQLite, but seeds 10,000 synthetic retry rows and deletes their parent directly in SQLite. It is not included in the recorded 29-case run; no passing result is claimed here. Byte-limit, concurrent admission and legacy-over-cap E2E acceptance remain pending; store boundary tests are supporting evidence only. The legacy over-cap case is an explicit exception to the hard limits until safe lifecycle deletion reduces existing data below them.

For an upgraded in-flight legacy row without accepted identity, a confirmed fenced failure must be recordable without increasing the row's charged size or the aggregate. Failure recording preserves the row's existing accepted identity rather than inventing recovery evidence; normal settlement remains available through the live fenced reservation. Failed-agent recovery still requires its independently recorded identity and safe-release proof. If a later admission cannot capture identity within capacity, it fails closed and the existing deadline still permits normal settlement.

### Issue #49 startup guard boundary

The scoped Claude-origin root startup guard ensures that a Claude root startup is admitted with an exact agent/session/epoch/generation/owner/operation guard before startup work begins, and that readiness or startup failure clears that exact guard in the same transaction as the lifecycle transition. It is separate from this spec's per-message durable retry admission: resolving a startup guard never clears an unresolved mail admission, and an existing startup guard blocks execution by every provider. The guard does not add generic post-ready turn phases. Acceptance for either contract remains pending independently.

### Scoped startup guard integration (draft)

A Claude root startup is admitted with an exact agent/session/epoch/generation/owner/operation guard before startup work. Admission records the selected provider independently of that guard, so a missing guard or later provider switch cannot prove success. Readiness or startup failure clears the exact guard and resolves the origin in the same transaction as the lifecycle and operation transition; rollback leaves all unchanged. A missing or mismatched origin or guard fails closed. Existing legacy intents remain blocking regardless of phase. Reset and alternate owner admission cannot bypass unresolved authority. On upgrade, previously starting Claude owners with a current session are quarantined if their outcome is unknown. Non-Claude startup does not install a Claude guard. Model snapshot validation and owner admission are one transaction. This is a draft contract; end-to-end acceptance remains pending.

### Live wake handoff

A live guard recheck must not lose queued mail when the active session is replaced or ends between its snapshot and steering. It revalidates the current session and, if no live session remains, hands the wake to serial dispatch. A retry already scheduled for an earlier guard error must not suppress an independent offer that needs serial dispatch. These handoffs do not bypass owner, retry-admission or submission fences. Browser-level acceptance for these interleavings remains pending.

### Startup owner admission rechecks (draft)

When startup admission conclusively rolls back due to a storage error, one coalesced recheck per incarnation continues at five-second intervals. After three failed attempts the manager logs a diagnostic, but does not stop rechecking or infer admission. It releases the serial, submission and admission gates while waiting and revalidates the launch, owner and selected model before acting. This admission-storage recovery is distinct from the bounded Claude mail startup retry policy above; it does not spend or override that policy. Operator termination takes precedence. An unknown Claude root owner-admission outcome returns no execution token. The manager schedules a delayed recheck without another offer; it does not infer rollback or execute from the unknown result. The recheck must run the complete atomic owner admission again, including current launch, session, generation, model and durable startup-guard fences. An existing Claude guard or unresolved Claude origin blocks admission even if the prior transaction committed; stale, changed or unreadable state cannot authorize execution. This rule does not authorize rechecking unknown admissions for other providers, or replaying uncertain Claude execution. Shutdown and restart do not turn uncertainty into proof. E2E acceptance remains pending.
